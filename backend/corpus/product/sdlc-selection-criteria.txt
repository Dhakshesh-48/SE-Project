Project condition → suitable SDLC model.

Waterfall: stable requirements, sequential delivery, and formal approvals.
V-Model: strict verification and validation with tests traced to each development stage.
Spiral: high uncertainty, technical risk, or severe failure consequences managed through risk-driven cycles.
Prototyping: unclear needs that benefit from early experiments and stakeholder feedback.
Iterative: large or complex systems that can be delivered and refined in increments.
Agile: explicitly evolving needs, frequent stakeholder feedback, and short delivery cycles.
RAD: small, low-risk work where rapid construction and delivery are the main constraints.

Assess these factors: requirement stability; regulatory criticality; security risk; project complexity; system size; legacy-system dependence; expected change frequency; need for continuous delivery; stakeholder availability; testing and documentation formality; budget and schedule pressure; need for formal verification; failure consequence. Judge requirement stability and expected change frequency from the project brief. Do not infer evolving needs from scheduled jobs, recurring reviews, or possible future regulation. Rate change frequency High only when the brief says needs are evolving.

Recommend exactly one of these seven models: Waterfall, V-Model, Spiral, Prototyping, Iterative, Agile, RAD.
